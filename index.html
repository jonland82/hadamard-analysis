A research program in exact combinatorics

Global order can predict the shape of what comes next—without telling us which next step is wise.

Hadamard matrices are rigid as complete objects. This project asks how that rigidity becomes visible during local construction, which parts survive a change of representation, and whether a correct regional forecast can reduce the work of exact search.

The sentence

Hadamard matrices reveal how global order can make local summaries predictable without making local decisions wise: the constraint state tells us what the next region should look like, but not which plausible region can belong to a complete whole.

The narrative arc

Three notes, each changing the meaning of “local”

The project began with next-entry prediction, found the more durable signal in regional constraint summaries, and then discovered that predictive information and algorithmic value are not the same thing.

The signal lives in the representative

Short contexts predict catalog representatives, but almost all of the gain collapses after equivalence-preserving row and column permutations.

First turn: printed adjacency is not an invariant local object.

The unfinished region carries a debt

Partial row sums and inner products fix the remaining inventories of signs and pairwise agreements. Those inventories predict regional composition under randomized presentations.

Second turn: locality belongs to the evolving constraint state.

Prediction is not a search policy

The regional score ranks known valid continuations strongly and helps against weak policies, but its advantage reverses against lexicographic search as construction depth grows.

Third turn: plausible shape is not residual completability.

The common mathematical language

From a complete orthogonal object to a partial search state

The same small set of quantities connects the probability experiments to the exact solver.

Hadamard structure

A matrix of order $d$ is Hadamard when

$$ H\in\{-1,+1\}^{d\times d}, \qquad HH^{\mathsf T}=dI_d. $$

After normalization, the first row and column are $+1$; every other row and column contains exactly $d/2$ signs of each kind.

Equivalent presentations

Permutations and sign changes preserve the complete object:

$$ K=D_rP_rHP_cD_c. $$

A statistic can be predictive in one representative without being invariant across its equivalence class.

Serialized context

For row-reset context $C_{i,j}^{(k)}$ and target $Y_{i,j}$, the smoothed forecast is

$$ \begin{aligned} \widehat q_k(c)&= \frac{N_c^++\tfrac12}{N_c^++N_c^-+1},\\ G_k&=\log 2-L_k. \end{aligned} $$

Positive $G_k$ is held-out gain over fair guessing, measured in nats per predicted sign.

The partial constraint state

After assigning $m$ entries of a new row $i$:

$$ \begin{aligned} R_i(m)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}H_{i\ell},\\ S_{ij}(m)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}H_{i\ell}H_{j\ell}, \qquad r=d-m. \end{aligned} $$

$R_i$ is balance debt; each $S_{ij}$ is orthogonality debt to an earlier row.

Exact regional correction laws

The remaining inventories are $P_i=(r-R_i)/2$ positive signs and $A_{ij}=(r-S_{ij})/2$ agreements. Under uniformly randomized remaining-coordinate order, the next block of size $b$ obeys

$$ \Pr(Y_b=q\mid R_i) =\frac{\binom{P_i}{q}\binom{r-P_i}{b-q}}{\binom rb}, \qquad \Pr(Z_{b,j}=q\mid S_{ij}) =\frac{\binom{A_{ij}}{q}\binom{r-A_{ij}}{b-q}}{\binom rb}. $$

These are exact marginal laws for regional composition—not a claim that the pairwise summaries are mutually independent.

Safe exact feasibility

A signed debt $u$ can be repaired by $r$ remaining signs exactly when

$$ |u|\le r, \qquad r-u\equiv0\pmod 2. $$

The solver applies this to $R_i$ and every $S_{ij}$. Probability orders surviving branches; it never proves one impossible.

The missing target

For state $s$ and feasible child block $x$, define

$$ V(s,x)=\mathbf 1\{\text{the subtree below }(s,x)\text{ contains a completion}\}. $$

Regional likelihood asks whether $x$ spends local debts plausibly. $V$ asks whether the choice can still belong to a complete whole.

The formal spine

Statements that carry the story

Each experiment is paired with a mathematical claim that says what should happen before data are examined—or what the data cannot establish.

Lemma

Normalization forces balance

Every Hadamard matrix is equivalent to a normalized one; every normalized nonfirst row and column has exactly $d/2$ entries of each sign.

Theorem

Exact one-step neutrality

Under uniform row-reset sampling of adjacent positions in any normalized Hadamard matrix, the next sign is conditionally fair given the preceding sign. First-order success would be a boundary artifact or a bug.

Proposition

Local counts are not equivalence invariants

For $k\ge2$, an equivalence-preserving column permutation can change row-wise context and target counts, even after normalization.

Lemma

Pooling invariance localizes the artifact

Whole-row permutation cannot change pooled row-reset counts; changing column order can. The column-reset dual holds by transposition.

Theorem

Regional correction law

Partial balance and pairwise inner products determine hypergeometric next-block laws under randomized exposure of the remaining coordinates.

Theorem

Ordering changes work, not correctness

With sound pruning and no budget, any policy that only permutes feasible children explores the same finite search tree and is complete for the same restricted completion problem.

Proposition

A perfect completability oracle is backtrack-free

If the root is satisfiable and a policy always chooses a child with $V(s,x)=1$, it reaches a solution without visiting a sibling.

Counterexample proposition

Strong offline ranking can search badly

For every $\varepsilon>0$ and $M$, an offline policy can be correct on at least $1-\varepsilon$ of successful-path states yet visit at least $M$ nodes before its first solution. A rare early error can own an enormous subtree.

The experimental program

A sequence of increasingly consequential tests

The sampling unit is a whole equivalence class, controls inherit the same split, and every claimed Hadamard matrix is checked exactly.

Data and provenance

The prediction studies use verified McKay equivalence-class representatives: $5$, $3$, $60$, and $487$ matrices at orders $16$, $20$, $24$, and $28$. Synthetic controls are generated from the same split structure. Exact-search instances reveal randomized satisfiable prefixes from verified matrices; the hidden completion is retained only as an audit witness and is never supplied to branch ordering.

Experiment A

Can a short left-to-right context predict the next sign on held-out matrices?

  • Orders $16,20,24,28$; context lengths $k=1,\ldots,12$.
  • Catalog representatives, randomized equivalents, balanced rows, and normalized IID signs.
  • $20$ paired repetitions, class-level train/test splits, bootstrap intervals, and $80$ leakage audits.

Experiment B

Which part of the representation creates the serialized signal?

  • Paired row/column traversal, same-axis and opposite-axis permutations, and normalization-anchor ablations.
  • The exact pooling invariances act as negative controls.
  • $7{,}680$ measurements and $40$ passing source-split audits.

Experiments C–D

Can the constraint state predict regional summaries after representation randomization?

  • Block sizes $b\in\{2,4,8\}$ and early, middle, and late construction stages.
  • Targets: block plus count and agreement count with the most-pressured prior row.
  • State laws versus fair blocks, serialized context, and state-plus-context; $11{,}520$ measurements.

Experiment E

Do regional laws rank the observed valid block among every immediately feasible alternative?

  • Enumerate all $2^b$ blocks, retain exact immediate feasibility, and remove forced or terminal states.
  • Compare random, balance-only, one-pair, pressured-pair, all-pair, and minimum-pressure scores.
  • $4{,}320$ policy measurements and $40$ passing audits.

Experiment F

Does the offline ranking reduce first-solution work in a complete exact solver?

  • Orders $16$ and $20$ with four or six hidden rows, plus full order-$12$ construction from two seed rows.
  • Identical exact pruning; only child order changes among six policies.
  • $2{,}070$ runs, fixed node/time budgets, solution digests, and independent exact verification.

What survived

The signal is real. The naive algorithmic conclusion is not.

The numbers establish a progression from representation-dependent entry prediction, to representation-resilient regional prediction, to a depth-dependent online reversal.

Supported

  • Catalog coordinate order carries transferable bounded-context signal.
  • Constraint state predicts regional summaries after equivalent randomization.
  • All-pair state improves on balance-only in $11/12$ main search conditions.
  • All $1{,}952$ returned solutions satisfy $HH^{\mathsf T}=dI_d$.

Not supported

  • The current marginal score is not a general replacement for lexicographic ordering.
  • High offline continuation percentile does not imply online acceleration.
  • The present evidence does not justify transfer to a mature SAT/CAS solver.
  • A budget cap is never treated as a proof of nonexistence.
Full order-$12$ construction result

From two seed rows, lexicographic and hybrid policies both solve all $15$ presentations. Lexicographic search has medians of $74$ nodes and $0.063$ seconds; the hybrid has medians of $1{,}385$ nodes and $1.083$ seconds. The paired hybrid-over-lex ratios are $7.59$ for nodes and $15.21$ for time.

The next mathematical object

Predict survival beyond the structural baseline

The first engineering gate is deliberately closed. Before moving to larger infrastructure, the project should test whether constraint state predicts residual branch survival, completion count, or failure of lexicographic structure.

$$ \text{global law} \Longrightarrow \text{predictable regional summary} \not\Longrightarrow \text{wise greedy action}, $$ $$ \text{so learn }V(s,x)\text{ or a controlled proxy for it.} $$

Candidate tests include bounded look-ahead survival, feasible-descendant counts, residual scoring on top of lexicographic order, and selection among algebraic construction templates. Only a policy that improves both nodes and net time against the strongest controlled baseline should graduate to SAT/CAS integration.
